# RotaRovers Martian Map: Science Stops & Geological Dossier

This document provides scientific background, geological interpretations, and operational field guides for the science stops defined in `content/stops.csv`. It serves as the companion dossier for team RotaRovers in the **2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge** (*Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map*).

---

## 1. Geological Setting: Sakarya Vallis (Gale Crater / Mount Sharp)

### Overview
The traverse area is located along **Sakarya Vallis** (informally known in prior MSL literature as the *Grand Canyon of Gale*), situated on the northwestern lower flanks of **Aeolis Mons (Mount Sharp)** within Gale Crater:
* **Footprint:** HiRISE DTM `DTEEC_006855_1750_007501_1750_A01` (~1.01 m/pixel resolution), spanning **137.1716° to 137.2895°E** and **-5.2217° to -4.9504°N**.
* **Regional Context:** Mount Sharp preserves a ~5 km-thick sedimentary sequence recording the major climatic transition of ancient Mars from warm, wet conditions (clays/phyllosilicates) to arid, evaporating conditions (sulfates and eolian sandstones).
* **The Canyon System:** Sakarya Vallis is a ~20–26 km long, up to 2 km wide, and ~350 m deep canyon that incised directly through the sulfate-bearing strata of Mount Sharp. It hosts extensive evidence of late-stage aqueous mass transport, inverted stream channels, and cemented debris flows.

## 2. Primary Walkable Traverse (Stops 1 to 6)

The primary science traverse is planned along a contiguous, walkable corridor (slopes strictly $\le 20^\circ$) connecting the lower canyon floor to the elevated eastern terrace.

```
[Stop 1: Inflow Terrace] 
       │ (~448 m)
[Stop 2: Ridge Foot] 
       │ (~568 m)
[Stop 3: East Wall Outcrop] 
       │ (~489 m)
[Stop 4: Debris Levees Overlook] 
       │ (~375 m)
[Stop 5: Canyon Chasm Overlook] 
       │ (~417 m)
[Stop 6: Northern Terrace & Basin Viewpoint]
```

* **Total Traverse Distance:** **2,297 meters (~2.30 km)**
* **Slope Regime:** Fully walkable ground ($\le 20^\circ$ slope), avoiding hazardous ridge crests and steep cliff pockets.
* **Estimated EVA Walking Time:** ~38–45 minutes walking at 0.9–1.0 m/s; ~2.5–3.0 hours total duration including 20–25 minutes of sampling per station.

### Detailed Stop Descriptions

#### Stop 1: Canyon Floor Inflow Terrace
* **Order:** `1`
* **Coordinates:** `137.2305°E, -5.1010°N`
* **Geological Feature:** Basal terrace of the southern canyon floor where fluvial incisive features originate.
* **Why Past Habitability:** Exposed bedrock preserves the basal transition into the stream deposit. Studying these lower strata reveals initial fluid dynamics, paleocurrent directions, and water chemistry prior to canyon deepening.
* **Suggested Field Actions:** Microscopic surface imaging of bedrock grains; APXS / Raman spectroscopy on in-situ rock faces; drill coring (0–10 cm).
* **Primary Citation:** [HiRISE Observation PSP_006855_1750](https://www.uahirise.org/PSP_006855_1750)

#### Stop 2: Inverted Channel Ridge Foot
* **Order:** `2`
* **Coordinates:** `137.2295°E, -5.0935°N`
* **Geological Feature:** Walkable valley floor at the immediate base of the sinuous inverted riverbed ridge.
* **Why Past Habitability:** Sinuous inverted channels form when stream-transported gravels and sands become cemented by minerals precipitated from liquid water (sulfates/silica). Later eolian deflation stripped away softer lateral rock, leaving the riverbed in positive relief. Astronauts sample freshly shed talus boulders from the riverbed without scaling hazardous steep slopes.
* **Suggested Field Actions:** Sampling eroded cobbles and cement matrix; handheld multispectral documentation of cement mineralogy.
* **Primary Citation:** [HiRISE Observation PSP_006855_1750](https://www.uahirise.org/PSP_006855_1750)

#### Stop 3: East Wall Stratified Outcrop
* **Order:** `3`
* **Coordinates:** `137.2355°E, -5.0860°N`
* **Geological Feature:** Finely rhythmic sedimentary beds exposed on the low-gradient toe of the eastern canyon wall.
* **Why Past Habitability:** These cyclic strata capture fluctuating lake levels, evaporitic shallow basins, and episodic sediment influx into ancient Gale crater. The rhythmic nature of the bedding provides a high-resolution chronostratigraphic archive of regional climate drying.
* **Suggested Field Actions:** Layer-by-layer stratigraphic logging; measuring lamina thickness; scraping weathered rind for subsurface sulfur/carbon isotopic screening.
* **Primary Citation:** [HiRISE Observation PSP_007501_1750](https://www.uahirise.org/PSP_007501_1750)

#### Stop 4: Debris Levees Overlook
* **Order:** `4`
* **Coordinates:** `137.2375°E, -5.0780°N`
* **Geological Feature:** Flat eastern terrace viewpoint overlooking canyon-floor debris ridges and lateral levees.
* **Why Past Habitability:** Numerical slope-stability models (Hughes et al., 2020) prove that mobilizing these coarse debris flows down low canyon slopes required high pore-water saturation and liquid groundwater exfiltration. Viewing and telephoto-surveying these features from the stable terrace documents wet debris-flow kinetics safely.
* **Suggested Field Actions:** Long-range telephoto imaging of levee structures; spectral surveying of floor polyhydrated sulfate signatures with mast-mounted spectrometers.
* **Primary Citation:** [Hughes et al., LPSC 2020 Abstract #2426](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2020/pdf/2426.pdf)

#### Stop 5: Canyon Chasm Overlook
* **Order:** `5`
* **Coordinates:** `137.2355°E, -5.0720°N`
* **Geological Feature:** Prominent walkable vantage point overlooking the deepest section of Sakarya Vallis and tributary alcoves.
* **Why Past Habitability:** Provides an uninhibited view into the cross-cutting relationships between sulfate strata and the main fluvial gorge. Polyhydrated sulfate spectral signatures indicate mineral hydration and groundwater interactions that persisted late into Martian history.
* **Suggested Field Actions:** Panoramic gigapixel stereo imaging; geological mapping of tributary junctions and canyon-wall groundwater sapping alcoves.
* **Primary Citation:** [Hughes et al., LPSC 2019 Abstract #3196](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2019/pdf/3196.pdf)

#### Stop 6: Northern Sulfate Terrace and Basin Viewpoint
* **Order:** `6`
* **Coordinates:** `137.2365°E, -5.0650°N`
* **Geological Feature:** Broad level plateau where Mount Sharp strata transition into the lower outwash fan and Gale floor.
* **Why Past Habitability:** This terminal terrace allows astronauts to synthesize the entire depositional watershed—from upper Mount Sharp source regions, through the incised canyon conduit, to the distal alluvial fan in northern Gale crater. Wind-exhumed yardangs here expose pristine sulfate bedrock.
* **Suggested Field Actions:** Regional traverse synthesis; rock core extraction from upper sulfate yardang unit; environmental sensor package deployment.
* **Primary Citation:** [USGS Gazetteer Feature 15941 (Sakarya Vallis)](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/15941)
